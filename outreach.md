# Direct outreach — the channel with no gatekeeper

Two angles. The audit email goes to businesses. The overflow email goes to other developers and small agencies. Both
work because almost nobody sends them.

---

## Angle 1 — the null-to-zero audit (to businesses)

**Who to send it to:** organizations that make decisions off recurring reports and don't have a data team. E-commerce
shops doing $1M-20M, marketing agencies that send client reports, bookkeeping and accounting firms, property management
companies, medical billing outfits, small SaaS with a metrics dashboard.

**Subject:** Quick question about your [monthly/client] reporting

> Hi [Name],
>
> Direct question: when a value is missing from one of your [sales exports /
> client reports / inventory feeds], do you know whether it shows up as blank —
> or as a zero?
>
> It matters more than it sounds. A missing measurement that quietly becomes 0
> pulls averages down, makes totals look complete, and produces a number that
> looks perfectly reasonable and is wrong. Most reporting pipelines do this
> silently and nobody finds out until a decision has already been made on it.
>
> I audit for exactly this. Fixed fee, about a week, and you get a written list
> of every place it's happening plus the fix. If it turns out your data is
> clean, I'll tell you that and we're done.
>
> Worth a 15-minute call?
>
> Ricky Erickson
> [phone] · [portfolio link]

**Why it works:** it names a specific failure the reader can immediately check, it doesn't ask them to imagine a
project, and the fixed fee removes the "how much will this balloon to" fear that kills most consulting cold emails.

---

## Angle 2 — overflow work (to developers and small agencies)

**Who to send it to:** solo developers and 2-10 person agencies who are visibly busy — the ones posting "booked through
Q4," the ones with a waitlist. They turn work away constantly and hate doing it.

**Subject:** Overflow help — Python / data / parsing

> Hi [Name],
>
> You look busy, which means you're probably turning things down.
>
> I take overflow. Specifically the unglamorous work most people don't want:
> parsing awkward file formats, data cleaning, scripts and automation, and the
> "this export is a mess" jobs. Python, SQL, and I'm comfortable across C, C++,
> Java, Ruby and JS if something needs porting.
>
> I work under your name if you prefer — your client never has to know I exist.
> Happy to take a small first job at a reduced rate so you can see the quality
> before you trust me with anything real.
>
> Interested?
>
> Ricky

**Why it works:** you're solving *their* problem (turning down money), not asking for a favour. Subcontracting also
skips the whole reviews-and-reputation problem, because their reputation carries the job. This is the single fastest
path to a first paid contract with no track record.

---

## Volume

Send 20 of each per week. Expect 1-3 replies per 20. That is normal and not a verdict on you — it's the arithmetic of
cold outreach, and it's still better odds than bidding against 239 people.

Track who you sent to and when. Follow up once after 5 days; the follow-up converts about as often as the original.
